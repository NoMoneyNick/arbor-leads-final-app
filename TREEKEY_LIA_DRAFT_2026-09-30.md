# TreeKey — Legitimate Interests Assessment (working draft)

Prepared: 30 September 2026. Owner: Nicholas Michael Secular, proposed controller trading as TreeKey. Confirm legal identity before approval; website material has also named Vector Data Labs.

**Status: assessment drafted, NOT approved legal clearance and NOT evidence that controls are implemented.** Prepared from the user's described model, Claude reports and the 27 September public-page audit. No code or production database inspected for this document. Live behaviour may differ. No processing or deployment is authorised by this document.

## Plain-English conclusion

There is a credible commercial purpose for offering one relevant postal introduction. Keeping the contractor from receiving household identities and allowing an easy objection materially improve the proposed model. However, public planning participation is not a request for marketing, and relevance alone does not settle necessity, reasonable expectations or transparency.

I cannot sign off the current collection-wide model on the evidence available. The principal unresolved issue is notifying people whose data is collected but whose opportunity is never purchased. Purging at 60 days does not establish an exception to the notification rules. Existing identifying marketplace descriptions also weaken the claimed safeguards until corrected.

This is a reason to resolve two specific issues, not a conclusion that every possible TreeKey model is unlawful.

## 1. Scope and processing map

Covered: collecting and assessing tree-work planning records, holding saleable opportunities, selecting suitable opportunities for contractors, producing a paid postal introduction, matching a voluntary homeowner response using a letter number, maintaining minimal dispatch evidence and respecting objections.

Separate assessment needed for contractor cold-email acquisition, customer-account marketing, financial tools and any later visitor tracking. The conclusions here do not authorise email or telephone marketing to householders.

Proposed flow:
1. Council public register -> restricted TreeKey collection and classification.
2. Buyer/public view -> non-identifying work category and sufficiently broad area only.
3. One purchasing contractor -> approved business letter; no household identity supplied.
4. Printer/postal processing -> recipient address and finished letter.
5. Homeowner chooses whether to contact contractor; letter number helps match the purchased introduction.
6. Live fulfilment identifiers deleted according to the retention plan; minimal separate evidence and objection matching retained.

Do not assume an applicant is the homeowner, that the applicant's correspondence address is the work site, or that consent/permission means a job remains available. Verify address suitability before mailing. Records may describe agents, neighbours, occupiers, corporate applicants or multiple households.

Reported collected fields: property address, applicant name where published, agent information, application reference, registration date, council and free-text work description. Phone/email collection for homeowners is not part of the stated model. Descriptions may contain third-party identities, medical reasons or other sensitive details even if the intended category is ordinary property information.

## 2. Purpose test — identified interests

TreeKey seeks revenue from relevant postal introductions. Contractors seek possible work within their stated services and area. These are specific commercial interests, not statutory tasks or a claim that homeowners requested the service.

A possible homeowner benefit is discovering a suitable contractor, but that is not assumed: they may have appointed someone, may not want contact, or may find the letter intrusive. Discounts do not override their preferences.

**Provisional result:** the commercial purpose is identifiable and can be legitimate in principle. Its actual method must pass the remaining tests and other applicable requirements. ICO guidance recognises potential use of legitimate interests for postal marketing, not automatic permission for all related collection. [S1, S2]

## 3. Necessity test — assess each use, not just the final letter

| Use | Assessment and least-intrusive recommendation |
|---|---|
| Finding relevant tree work | A limited record can help distinguish tree work from unrelated planning. Document actual filters and exclude irrelevant applications promptly. |
| Mailing an introduction | A deliverable address is required, but an applicant name usually is not if the letter addresses the property owner/occupier. Prefer not retaining unnecessary names. |
| Avoiding duplicates | A restricted reference or keyed matching token may be needed. A public council reference need not be disclosed to buyers. |
| Contractor selection | Broad area, work type and a safe structured summary should usually suffice. Street names, source links, names and verbatim narratives are not necessary for the stated no-identity offer. |
| Agent information | Retain only what is genuinely needed to avoid already-represented/unsuitable work; prefer a classification over long-term personal agent details. |
| Holding unpurchased opportunities | The user chose 56-day sale eligibility and 60-day deletion from council registration. That is a business rule, not proof that retaining every field for that long is necessary. Explain the four-day gap and strip fields sooner where their purpose ends. |
| Post-dispatch retention | The 72-hour window is a proposed short operational reconciliation period. Evidence of actual need and deletion coverage is still required. It is not a statutory retention period. |
| Customer evidence | A letter number, approved contractor-content version, status and payment link can often provide useful evidence without preserving the addressed PDF. Linkable identifiers remain personal data. |

Alternatives considered: generic unaddressed advertising; opt-in homeowner requests; on-demand retrieval after contractor interest; and retaining only aggregate/non-identifying information until needed. These have different commercial effectiveness and cost. On-demand retrieval does not eliminate processing at retrieval; an address-free record can still be personal data if references/descriptions identify someone. Document why the selected approach is proportionate rather than dismissing alternatives solely because they earn less revenue.

**Provisional result:** a minimal, restricted address-based mailing process has a stronger necessity case than bulk personal-data retention or a public raw-description directory. Current raw-description exposure and indefinite raw identifiers are not justified by this draft.

## 4. Balancing test — effects on people

Factors in TreeKey's favour: a limited relevant physical letter, voluntary response, no homeowner cold calls/email, no sale of household identity to contractors, brief operational retention and practical objection handling. These are intended safeguards, not all independently verified.

Factors against: statutory publication is often unavoidable; the person may not reasonably expect a separate commercial aggregator; some records are old or concern completed work; the addressee may be wrong; repeated applications can produce repeated contact; names/addresses in public summaries expose more than one letter would; free text may contain vulnerabilities or sensitive circumstances. Combined public datasets can increase identifiability.

Required safeguards before a favourable final finding:
- Limit to relevant, current, deliverable and non-sensitive opportunities; omit unsuitable emergencies and unnecessary personal fields.
- No identifying public/buyer descriptions, raw references, maps, portal links or downloadable recipient material. Generic preview data only.
- Check duplicates and objections before dispatch, not only at collection. Distinguish one-letter-per-application from household-wide opt-outs.
- Clear commercial identity and purpose; no claim of council endorsement, requested quotation or verified availability.
- Prominent simple objection route, no account required, no mandatory letter number. Do not continue marketing after objection. A minimal separate matching record may be retained to honour it. [S3]
- Finalise retention, processor agreements and access restrictions; monitor whether deletion actually happens.
- Record complaints, misaddressed letters and suppression failures. Proposed review: one month after a limited authorised launch, then quarterly and after material change/incident. These intervals are recommendations, not existing operations.

**Provisional result:** potentially defensible for the constrained process, but not an unconditional pass. No homeowner research, complaint history or production control evidence has been supplied to establish actual expectations and effects.

## 5. Transparency / Article 14 — unresolved gate

ICO guidance requires privacy information after collection from other sources within a reasonable period, no later than one month, and earlier where the first communication/disclosure requires it. Public availability does not remove the duty. If relying on impossibility or disproportionate effort, the ICO calls for a DPIA and documented justification. [S4, S5]

Application to TreeKey: a letter to purchased prospects might inform some people promptly; it does not account for people never mailed or mailed late. The collection clock differs from the council-registration clock. Earlier disclosure to a provider may also matter. A website notice alone is not assumed sufficient. Deletion before/after a deadline is not an automatic exemption.

Evidence needed for the decision: monthly unique people/households collected; collection dates and proposed notice dates; purchased proportion; actual cost of a plain privacy notification (not assumed identical to the sales letter); who could be contacted, including applicants/agents not resident at the site; privacy impact; alternatives; and measures protecting people if an exception is proposed. Financial pressure alone is not a demonstrated exemption.

### Options for review, not pre-approved workarounds

A. Provide notices to all affected people on the required timetable; quantify cost and whether the business remains viable.
B. Restrict/minimise collection and obtain genuinely non-identifying data until needed; assess the remaining processing honestly.
C. Seek a reasoned exception assessment supported by a DPIA and evidence. Do not publish or rely on it merely because a document has been written.

**Current decision: no exception established.** If none applies, purchased-only notification cannot be treated as the settled compliant model. Obtain a scoped UK data-protection review before relying on that model. The ICO can provide guidance but does not provide a blanket business approval.

## 6. Roles, providers and related checks

Confirm TreeKey's controller identity and each provider's role. Intelliprint, hosting/database, storage and email systems may handle recipient data. Obtain applicable processor terms, subprocessor/location information and transfer safeguards; do not copy unverified claims from marketing pages. A contractor may process data independently when a homeowner contacts them; this does not automatically determine every party's legal role in arranging the marketing.

Review DPIA necessity beyond the Article 14 issue if scale, profiling, matching or unexpected reuse create relevant risks. No special-category processing or solely automated decisions with significant effects are authorised by this assessment. Do not assume public-access rights equal unrestricted database licensing or permission to bypass portal protections.
