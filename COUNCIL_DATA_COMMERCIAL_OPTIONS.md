# Commercial products from council and company data

## Recommendation

Start with a paid, evidence-backed prospect report for one construction supply-chain niche. Combine recent council records with the companies involved, their relevant project histories and sourced business contact details. Deliver a preview, take payment through the existing Stripe checkout and release a dated CSV and readable report. This is the shortest route from the existing assets to a concrete product that a purchaser can inspect.

With a completed HMO scraper also available, the first recommended offer is an HMO project-and-company report for a specialist supplier. A regional list of active architectural practices or developer companies is the alternative if the HMO records do not support good commercial-party matches. An application proves involvement in a proposed project; it does not prove that the recipient wants a quote, lacks an incumbent supplier or will proceed with construction.

This is a ranked commercial assessment, not a claim that new customers or first-week profits have been established. All proposed prices are experiments. No outbound campaign, checkout change or live data export has been performed. Research sources were checked on 13 September 2026.

## Available assets and boundaries

The available stack is reported to include a daily council crawler/scraper, a completed HMO scraper, a Companies House filtering and website/email discovery pipeline, entity graph, marketplace, notification, outreach, reporting, scoring, semantic, workflow and escrow engines, plus Stripe checkout. Google Workspace is available and no new monthly software subscription is desired. These components are optional assets: use them when they reduce work or improve the product, not to make every idea consume the full stack.

The assessment uses those capabilities as the starting point. Existing code and model review scores do not, by themselves, establish national live coverage, historical completeness, document extraction, marginal operating cost or successful production integration. A targeted inspection of the local mesh_scrapers.py found tree/HMO search filters and several adapters excluding decided cases. That is not a full audit and does not establish the scope of any other scraper. Products requiring all-sector history, decision notices or licensing records therefore have explicit dependencies below.

The stated data licence has not been identified. If it refers to ICO registration, that is different from permission to reuse every dataset or contact every person. Public business contact data remains subject to applicable direct-marketing and data-protection rules. Separate the public company record from the independently discovered email and keep provenance for both. Companies House confirms that registered company email addresses are not public. [1][2][3]

TreeKey's existing exclusive inventory must be excluded from new reports, samples and reseller feeds wherever reuse would breach the original promise. A different brand or trade category does not automatically make reuse permissible. These products should use a separately eligible dataset. No exclusivity should be promised for public information beyond the distribution controls actually offered.

## Demand and competition

There is an established buyer category for construction intelligence. Barbour ABI markets project data to manufacturers, merchants, hire businesses and recruiters, and describes manual research of project roles and progress. NBS and Glenigan offer manufacturers a connection between specifications and project intelligence. These are direct examples of commercial positioning, not independently audited customer returns. [4][5]

The addressable activity is substantial: England's district-level planning authorities received 78,800 applications in July–September 2025. That is an official historical activity count for England, not a count of ready-to-buy projects, current scraper records or UK-wide opportunities. [6]

Basic data is already competitive. Endole advertises filtered business lists, contact information and database refresh functions. FreeBuilderLeads provides a free planning-based builder search. A new offer must therefore do more than return names selected by SIC code or a list of extensions. [7][8]

## List A: six small commercial offers

These are intentionally compatible offers built on a shared backend. They are not six unrelated software businesses. Launch one, then add an adjacent offer only after actual purchase behaviour supports it.

### A1. HMO project-and-company packs — first choice

**Buyer:** HMO furniture suppliers, specialist conversion contractors and HMO-focused letting or management companies. Furniture suppliers are the first buyer segment to examine because they explicitly sell room and property furnishing packages; management companies are a secondary segment because an existing operator may already manage its properties internally.

**Product:** a regionally scoped report of relevant HMO activity, grouped by corporate applicant or operator where the evidence supports that role. Include project address, application reference and date, proposal/status, proposed room count only when explicitly stated, company identity, sourced business contact and why the project matches the buyer's service. Each role must reflect its source: applicant, licence holder, manager, agent and owner are not interchangeable.

**Price hypothesis:** £149 for a dated initial report, with the actual qualifying record and company counts visible before checkout. A £99 monthly refresh is a subsequent test only if new activity is sufficient and customers want it. A report with ten projects belonging to one company must not be marketed as ten independent buyer relationships.

**Reuse:** HMO scraper, company/email discovery, entity graph, semantic filtering, scoring, reporting, workflow and Stripe. Notifications can deliver refreshes. An existing marketplace can provide download access but is optional. Escrow adds no necessary value to a fixed report purchase.

**Evidence:** HMO Furniture and Instore Direct market furniture packages specifically to this audience. Planning Signal already sells access to HMO planning applications, so raw HMO alerts are not unique. The proposed additional value is reliable company matching, recent project grouping, contact provenance and supplier-specific relevance. Actual demand for this combined report remains untested. [17][18][19]

**Failure to catch:** a planning application is not an open furniture order or proof of construction completion. A licence may concern an existing property with no new furnishing requirement. Separate new conversion proposals, extensions, existing-use records and licensing events. If the scraper covers licensing only, market it as an operator/contact research report rather than a new-conversion pipeline. Prefer corporate business records for the first offer; do not turn publicly listed private landlords into an indiscriminate email list.

**Dependencies:** confirm whether the HMO scraper collects planning, licensing or both. Reliable history and contact matching are more important than a large raw address count. This offer is first because the specialised collection is already reported complete, not because HMO leads are presumed more profitable than all alternatives.

### A2. Active business-account prospect packs

**Buyer:** structural engineering firms, specialist glazing suppliers, drainage consultants or other suppliers seeking repeat relationships with architectural practices and developers.

**Product:** a dated regional list of relevant practices or companies with recent projects matching an exact brief. For instance, architectural practices appearing as agents on proposed multi-unit residential developments in a specified area. Each account includes the business identity, company number where confidently matched, website, sourced business email, recent project references, stated role and an explanation of relevance.

**Commercial reason to buy:** the customer saves the work of distinguishing firms with relevant recent activity from a general directory. One relationship could produce repeat opportunities, although neither relationship formation nor purchasing is guaranteed.

**Reuse:** council and company scrapers; entity graph for name matching and project grouping; semantic engine for project classification; scoring for prioritisation; reporting, workflow and Stripe for delivery. The outreach engine can identify and prepare approaches to potential purchasers once sending is authorised.

**Smallest build:** one preset search, a reproducible account-grouping query, a preview and an export. No map, bidding system or customer CRM integration is necessary.

**Price hypothesis:** £149 for an initial clearly scoped report. Display the actual qualifying account count and snapshot date before checkout rather than guaranteeing an arbitrary number. £99–£249 is a reasonable range to test against report depth, not a validated market-clearing price.

**Failure to catch:** architects can already have preferred engineering partners; company names can collide; multiple applications can describe the same project. Group revisions and amendments, distinguish applications from projects, and provide source evidence. Ranking should describe observed activity and suitability, not willingness to buy. This category overlaps established business and construction data providers. [4][7]

### A3. A specialist project bulletin

**Buyer:** one narrowly defined trade or consultancy. Good candidates for examination are commercial kitchen extraction installers, acoustic consultants and drainage designers.

**Product:** recent proposals or notices explicitly mentioning the buyer's work, with the original wording, stage, project parties and source. An extraction installer receives projects proposing kitchen extraction or ventilation; an acoustic consultant receives records explicitly requesting acoustic work. A broad reference to a restaurant is weaker than a specific statement of the work.

**Reuse:** crawler, scraper, semantic and scoring engines, entity graph, company/email enrichment, reporting, notifications, workflow and Stripe.

**Smallest build:** a daily filter and a weekly report. Proposed opening price £79–£149 per month for a defined geography and specialty, after the customer sees actual recent volume. A paid one-off report can precede recurring billing.

**Dependencies:** proposal-text matches can be relatively simple. Consultant responses, decision conditions and previously submitted specialist reports require document collection and reliable linking. Planning conditions can specify required steps, but a condition is not evidence that a supplier has not already been appointed. English guidance must not be presented as a uniform rule for every UK nation. [9]

**Failure to catch:** a submitted acoustic or drainage report may identify the incumbent consultant. Show the incumbent where visible and classify supplier availability as unknown unless verified. Low-volume areas need honest previews rather than promised monthly lead counts. Broad incumbents already sell related project intelligence. [4]

### A4. Customer-list enrichment with current project activity

**Buyer:** a construction supplier, consultancy or specialist marketing agency with an existing company list.

**Product:** the buyer uploads company names or numbers. Return resolved identities, current sourced business contacts, and recent relevant planning involvement. Include a separate unresolved list instead of inventing missing matches. This can help a sales team prioritise accounts it already knows.

**Reuse:** company scraper, entity graph, council scraper, scoring, reporting and workflow, with Stripe payment. Semantic processing is useful for distinguishing business roles and project types.

**Price hypothesis:** £99–£299 for a bounded batch with an agreed input ceiling and output schema. Price the transformation delivered, not a guarantee that every row will have a discoverable email. Show a small sample match before purchase.

**Failure to catch:** registered addresses are not necessarily trading locations; company directors and accountants must not be treated as project decision-makers. Uploads add a data-security obligation. A CSV upload/export is smaller than direct CRM access. Endole explicitly offers database refreshing, so the differentiator here is council-project enrichment. [7]

### A5. A named-company project watch

**Buyer:** a product supplier or consultancy monitoring existing customers, selected prospects or corporate competitors.

**Product:** alert when a watched company appears in a newly observed application, or when a tracked application changes status. Explain the connection and link to the evidence. The customer controls which companies are relevant, reducing speculative lead scoring.

**Reuse:** entity graph, crawler, change-detection workflow, notification engine, reporting and Stripe. Scoring can separate high-confidence identity matches from ambiguous ones.

**Price hypothesis:** £49–£99 per month for a modest, defined watchlist. Maintain separate limits for companies and tracked cases. Customer-defined watchlists may produce useful recurring value even when there are no newly discovered prospects to sell.

**Dependencies:** daily scans of new records are enough for some new-appearance alerts. Decision/document alerts require revisiting existing applications and storing previous states. If history is unavailable, do not present an old record first encountered today as a new application.

**Failure to catch:** a corporate group can apply through subsidiaries or an agent. Clearly disclose missed-match and uncertain-match limitations. This offer has lower initial trust requirements than managing a customer's systems, but reliability still matters. Portfolio and planning-monitoring competitors include Plota and PlanTrack. [10][11]

### A6. An agency-branded weekly data feed

**Buyer:** a specialist construction marketing agency or consultant with an existing audience of paying business clients.

**Product:** a fixed-scope, dated report or CSV that the agency can distribute under a specified internal-use or redistribution licence. Start with one region and specialty, a branded report header and a delivery schedule.

**Reuse:** the same filtering and enrichment pipeline as the prospect packs and specialist bulletin, plus marketplace access control if useful, reporting, workflow, notifications and Stripe. Outreach can locate relevant incorporated agencies and prepare evidence-led offers.

**Price hypothesis:** £199–£399 per month for a tightly limited feed. This is a proposed wholesale experiment, not a claim that agencies will pay it. Redistribution rights, number of end clients and record scope materially affect pricing.

**Commercial attraction:** one purchaser already has distribution. This can reduce the number of direct end-customer relationships required, although the agency will still need convincing evidence of quality.

**Failure to catch:** unrestricted resale can undercut TreeKey and create more support than expected. Check dataset rights and existing exclusivity before creating a reseller offer. An agency can also buy from established data providers. Begin with a sample and a narrowly defined permitted use rather than a broad promise to power its entire service.

## List B: seven larger businesses

### B1. Specialist construction supply intelligence

Develop the specialist bulletin into a platform for one supplier category, then expand. Identify relevant project requirements, group applications into projects, track the companies and surface changes at useful stages. Customers pay for searching, saved accounts, alerts and export.

The moat would be accurate role matching, source-backed requirement extraction, stage history and customer relevance feedback. It is not merely nationwide scraping. Product specification and construction intelligence competitors demonstrate an established category, while also setting a high quality bar. [4][5]

Uses crawler, scraper, semantic engine, entity graph, scoring, reporting, notifications, workflow, marketplace/access control and Stripe. Outreach supports acquisition. Escrow has no initial role. Additional work includes document pipelines, history, feedback and substantial quality evaluation.

**Ranking:** strongest larger direction because a small paid report can test the exact customer problem first.

### B2. New commercial premises intelligence

Connect planning changes, premises licences and company records to identify proposed restaurants, bars, gyms, nurseries or other premises. Sell differentiated views to fit-out contractors, ventilation installers, equipment vendors, signage firms and commercial suppliers.

An application, licence variation, ownership transfer and confirmed opening must be distinct events. In particular, a new company is not proof of a new venue. The graph resolves trading names, legal entities and physical sites; the semantic engine extracts the event; scoring prioritises the fit for each supplier.

LicenceLeads already markets London licensing-based supplier intelligence from £99 per month. Its public offering is a direct competitive benchmark, not proof of earnings or its advertised lead times. [12]

Uses both scrapers, crawler, entity graph, semantic, scoring, marketplace, notifications, reporting, workflow, outreach and Stripe. No escrow is needed to sell intelligence. Requires licensing-register coverage if not already collected, site/entity resolution and corroborating updates. Different venue types need different sources.

**Ranking:** strong larger candidate, conditional on source coverage and accurate distinction between proposals and openings.

### B3. Planning requirement-to-provider marketplace

Extract a stated requirement such as acoustic assessment, drainage design or ecological work, then let the applicant or agent confirm a scope and request suitable providers. Providers quote and deliver through a workflow. Revenue could be a clearly disclosed platform fee or provider subscription.

This is the idea where the marketplace and escrow engines become central, alongside semantic extraction, entity matching, scoring, notifications, reporting, outreach and workflow. Stripe supports the payment implementation only within the payment provider's supported product and arrangement; an escrow engine is not proof of a production-ready or legally permitted funds-custody service.

The critical conversion is from a public record to a buyer who actually wants a service. Scraping does not solve that. Some requirements already have appointed providers, and quotations and disputes need human participation. Start from confirmed requests and provider matching; payment holding is a later product/legal dependency.

**Ranking:** credible higher-value expansion after evidence of genuine requests, not a quick-money launch. The existence of planning conditions supports the trigger, not an unfilled-job count. [9]

### B4. Developer and corporate-project relationship intelligence

Build an evidence-linked graph of corporate applicants, agents, related companies and their project histories. Buyers could be suppliers seeking repeat developer clients, commercial agents or specialist research teams.

The first product answers factual questions: which companies repeatedly appear in a target project category, who acts for them and which regions they operate in. A later site-sourcing product needs additional ownership, boundaries and constraint data. A company association does not establish land ownership, control, intent to sell or financial capacity.

LandInsight and Searchland already combine planning with ownership and property information. Their breadth explains why a scraper plus Companies House alone should not be positioned as a complete replacement. [13][14]

Uses entity graph most heavily, plus both scrapers, crawler, semantic, scoring, reporting, workflow, notifications, access control, outreach and Stripe. Escrow is unnecessary. Entity disambiguation, additional licences and historical completeness are the largest dependencies.

**Ranking:** attractive if the business-account prospect pack demonstrates repeat demand; more difficult than the first two larger options.

### B5. A planning-and-company data API

Sell a documented feed of normalised applications, entities and changes to software companies, niche information services and agencies. Start with one feed and a declared coverage list. Customers pay for usable data delivery and maintenance rather than a consumer-facing lead marketplace.

Planning.org.uk and Searchland publish APIs, establishing direct competition. A defensible initial offer needs a specific advantage such as a better-supported portal, cleaner project grouping, clearer provenance or a missing event type. National coverage alone should not be assumed unique. [15][16]

Uses crawler, scrapers, graph, semantic, scoring, workflow, notifications/webhooks, reporting, access control and Stripe. Outreach targets integrations and data buyers. Escrow is not needed. API keys, metering, versioning, incremental updates, deletion propagation, redistribution terms and support become product work.

**Ranking:** good infrastructure business, but integration sales and reliability expectations make it slower than selling a report. An initial downloadable feed can test it without prematurely building a general API.

### B6. Planning change monitoring for corporate property portfolios

Customers provide sites or assets, then receive relevant surrounding planning activity and tracked case changes. A commercial operator might watch proposed competing sites; a property manager might monitor nearby major development. The product sells continuing awareness rather than new sales prospects.

Plota already offers portfolio and estate planning monitoring. The narrow opening is a clearer relevance model or workflow for one operator type, rather than generic postcode alerts. [10]

Uses crawler, scraper, spatial matching, entity graph, semantic filtering, scoring, notifications, reporting, workflow, marketplace/access control and Stripe. Outreach supports acquisition; escrow is unnecessary. Reliable geocoding and boundary interpretation may require additional data. Corporate names and registered offices cannot substitute for a site's location.

**Ranking:** a distinct recurring use case; valuable only if false positives and coverage gaps are communicated well. Start as a watchlist report before developing portfolio software.

### B7. HMO development and operator intelligence

Expand the initial HMO report into a searchable history of HMO-related properties, company participants and observed changes. Customers could search corporate operators, follow proposed conversions, identify repeat applicants and receive supplier-specific alerts. The additional value comes from linking records that are otherwise scattered, rather than presenting a static licence map.

Use the HMO and company scrapers, graph, semantic classification, scoring, notifications, reporting, workflow, Stripe and optional marketplace access. Outreach supports acquisition; escrow becomes relevant only if a later service actually arranges transactions. No tool needs to be included simply because it exists.

Planning Signal is a direct competitor for HMO planning leads. General planning/ownership tools are adjacent competitors. HMO property-management and compliance software is a different category: building rent collection, tenant messaging and legal-document workflows would increase scope beyond the initial data advantage. [19][13][14]

A property appearing in planning or licensing data does not establish an available room, an owner willing to sell, an unappointed manager or a legal compliance failure. Do not promise yields without rent and cost data, and do not infer non-compliance from a missing record. HMO licensing fields and rules also vary by jurisdiction. [20]

**Ranking:** strongest specialised expansion if the initial HMO report attracts repeat purchases. It has a clearer path from current assets than starting a general property portal.

## How to turn the first offer into a purchasable product

Choose one buyer and one observable criterion. The first hypothesis is an HMO furniture supplier purchasing a report of proposed HMO conversions and the corporate businesses connected to them. Existing licensed properties belong in a separate operator research report. A structural engineering firm purchasing active architectural-practice data is the fallback if HMO company matching or project coverage is weak. These are market-entry hypotheses, not evidence that anyone lacks an incumbent supplier.

First create an actual report from the eligible dataset. Resolve company identity, group repeat applications and show source dates. Publish the exact count, region, filters and included fields. Prepare a handful of representative source-linked preview rows. If the report is too small or its matching is unreliable, change scope before accepting payment.

Use the company scraper to create a separate list of potential purchasers, based on the services actually described on their business websites as well as company filters. Those contacts are sales prospects for the report; they are not the same population as the accounts in the report. Email discovery, a valid domain and a delivered email are different observations and should not share a single unqualified verified label.

The minimum customer journey is offer page, preview, Stripe checkout and secure report delivery. A workflow should verify the payment webhook, bind the purchased report version to the order, deliver once and record errors for review. The existing marketplace UI is optional. This first product can be fulfilled from a prepared snapshot rather than making the buyer wait for live scraping.

The first outreach should show the specific relevance of the sample and the fixed deliverable, subject to the applicable contact rules and authorisation to send. Neither testimonials nor a sales call need be mandatory checkout steps. However, initial customer acquisition is not proven autonomous just because an outreach engine exists.

A possible implementation sequence is two days to inspect and assemble an eligible sample, two days to connect the offer and delivery, and the remaining part of a first week to quality-check and present the offer. This is conditional engineering sequencing, not a promised sale date. Missing all-sector records, licences, entity matches or payment reliability could change it materially.

At a £149 selling price, five purchases produce £745 gross revenue; ten produce £1,490. These figures are deliberately not labelled profit. Payment fees, marginal scraping/email/AI costs, refunds, acquisition time and support must be deducted. Reusing existing engines can reduce setup work, but does not make runtime inference or cloud capacity free. Inspect whether the semantic and scoring engines call paid services; simple classification and report assembly may be implemented without those calls.

After the first buyers use the report, the most useful feedback is factual: which rows were wrong, which companies were relevant, which fields were missing and whether a refresh was purchased. A second paid order is stronger evidence of repeat value than a favourable sample review. If repeat demand emerges, prospect reports can become named-company watches or agency feeds, then specialised supplier or HMO intelligence. If the offer gets no purchases, separate lack of qualified exposure from poor conversion before deciding whether the product or distribution failed.
